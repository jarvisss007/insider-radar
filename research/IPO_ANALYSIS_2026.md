## 1. The 2026 IPO market

The window reopened hard in 2026 — the strongest issuance since 2021:

- **~$251B raised across 86 US deals through late July 2026** — already far past
  Goldman's pre-year projection of a record ~$160B for the full year.
- IPOs raising >$50M were up **~+20% on average through June 30**.
- The boom is **concentrated**: SpaceX alone accounts for roughly a third of all US
  IPO proceeds this year. Twelve $1B+ deals carried the tape. This is a mega-deal
  market, not a broad one — closer in structure to 2021's blank-check froth than to
  1999's breadth, though the issuers this time have (mostly) real revenue.

Drivers cited by street research: lower rates, the private-market valuation gap
closing, and a large backlog of PE/VC-backed companies under pressure to exit.

## 2. Class of 2026 — what actually listed

### Cerebras (CBRS) — the season opener
- IPO **2026-05-14**, priced **$185** vs an original $115–125 range, raised **$5.55B**,
  opened at **$385 (+108%)**. Now ~**$46B** market cap vs ~$23B private mark in early 2026.
- Read: the +108% pop is the classic sign of a starved market — and classic
  "money left on the table" (the company sold $5.5B of stock worth ~$11B at the open).

### SpaceX (SPCX) — the biggest IPO in history
- IPO **2026-06-12** on Nasdaq: **$135/share**, 555M+ shares, **$75B raised** — the
  largest IPO ever. Day 1: **+19%**, closed $160.95, intraday >+30% and briefly a
  **>$2.25T** market value.
- Since: added to the Nasdaq-100 in early July, then **slid below its debut and even
  below the $135 offer**, reclaiming the offer price only on Aug 10. As of **Aug 17:
  ~$140, ~$1.85T market cap** — +3.7% vs offer, **−13% vs the day-one close**.
- Lesson already delivered: two months in, everyone who bought day-one enthusiasm is
  down; only allocation-getters are up. That is the base rate working in real time.

## 3. Anthropic — the one everyone asks about

**Facts (company-confirmed):**
- **2026-06-01:** Anthropic confidentially submitted a **draft S-1** to the SEC —
  announced on its own newsroom. Its own wording: pending SEC review, "this gives us
  the **option** to pursue an initial public offering."
- **May 2026:** closed a **$65B Series H at a $965B post-money valuation** — days
  before the S-1.
- **Not set:** price, share count, ticker, exchange, underwriters, date.

**Estimates (third-party, lower confidence):**
- Trackers put ARR in the vicinity of **$70B** (unaudited, third-party estimate).
- Forecasters (futuresearch) put the **median listing ~Nov 30, 2026** at a
  ~$1.18T first-day cap; prediction markets price **~76% odds of a 2026 listing**.

**Analysis.** At ~$965B on ~$70B ARR the private mark is ~14x forward revenue —
rich but not absurd by AI-era standards (and a different universe from 1999, when
issuers had no revenue at all). The real questions for a public Anthropic are the
same as for every AI lab: compute capex intensity, margin path, customer
concentration in a handful of hyperscaler/enterprise deals, and whether model-layer
pricing power survives open-weight competition. The S-1, when public, is the first
time anyone outside gets audited numbers — **wait for it; every revenue figure in
circulation today is a leak or an estimate.**

Timing risk cuts both ways: a November listing would land in the same window the
market must also digest a possible OpenAI deal — two near-$1T AI offerings compete
for the same marginal dollar.

## 5. History lesson — the dot-com IPO era (1995–2002)

The full deal table lives in `docs/data/ipos.json`. Headline numbers:

- **476 US IPOs in 1999**; average **first-day return +71% in 1999** and +56% in
  2000, versus ~15% in the ordinary 1990s (Ritter data). Issuers left **~$65B** on
  the table across 1999–2000.
- **Nasdaq peaked at 5,048.62 on 2000-03-10** and fell **~78%** to the Oct-2002
  trough. IPO issuance collapsed to **~80 deals in 2001**.
- The era's poster children: Netscape (+108%, Aug '95 — the starting gun),
  theglobe.com (+606%, Nov '98 — zero by '01), VA Linux (**+698%**, Dec '99 — the
  all-time record pop, −90% within a year), Pets.com (IPO Feb '00, liquidated
  **268 days later**), Webvan (~$8B at debut, bankrupt Jul '01), Palm (Mar '00 —
  briefly priced above what its parent 3Com's stake implied, an arithmetic
  impossibility that marked the top almost to the day).
- The survivors are the hard part: **Amazon fell ~95%** in 2000–01 before becoming
  one of the best stocks in history; **Priceline fell ~99%** and still ended up a
  giant (Booking). In 1999 Amazon and Pets.com looked like the *same bet*.
  Survivorship was not identifiable in real time.

**Transferable lessons for 2026:**
1. First-day pops are a cost paid by issuers and captured by allocation-getters;
   chasing the close is the systematically worst entry.
2. Being right about the technology (the internet DID change everything) did not
   rescue buyers who paid any price for it. Same test now applies to "AI changes
   everything" — true thesis, unresolved price.
3. Windows slam shut faster than they open (476 → ~80 deals in two years).
4. Concentrated narrative vintages underperform for years after the peak.
5. 2026 differs from 1999 in one big way: today's mega-issuers have tens of
   billions in revenue. It rhymes in another: valuation multiples that assume
   flawless execution, and issuance accelerating into enthusiasm.

## 6. Base rates (the boring truth)

- Over the last ~30 years, buying US IPOs at the **day-one close**: mean 1-year
  return **+10.4%**, median **−4.7%** (LPL, 2026). A few giant winners carry the
  mean; the typical deal loses.
- Ritter's long-run work (1980–present): IPOs underperform comparable stocks by
  roughly **2–3%/yr over the first 3 years** on average.
- Mechanical calendar that moves these stocks regardless of fundamentals:
  **quiet-period end** (~25–40 days, first analyst ratings — mostly from
  underwriters), **index inclusion** (forced passive buying), **lockup expiry**
  (~180 days — insider supply; for SpaceX that's ~**Dec 2026**).

## 7. SpaceX forward view — scenarios, not predictions

- **Bull:** Starlink compounding, Starship cadence, passive flows, scarcity premium
  as *the* listed space asset. Sell-side 12-mo consensus ~$227 — discount it
  heavily; most raters banked fees on the deal.
- **Base:** at ~$1.85T the price already embeds decades of execution; base rates say
  mega-IPOs chop around the offer for quarters; Dec lockup adds supply. $110–170 range.
- **Bear:** Starship setback, Kuiper competition, key-person/political volatility.
  July showed the floor is soft; below $110 revisits the lows.
